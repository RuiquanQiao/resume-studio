# Checking a role, a company and an offer for traps

A new graduate rarely sees the trap in a posting: they have never read a contract, don't know what is normal,
and are afraid to ask. This skill is the experienced friend who reads it first. It finds **signals**, each with
its evidence, says why it matters, and says what to ask or check. It never decides for the user, and it never
calls a company bad on a hunch.

## 1. How to report what you find

Every finding has four parts, in a line or two:

- **what**: the signal, quoted from the posting, the offer or a source ("底薪 3000 + 提成", "试用期 6 个月");
- **why it matters**: what it usually means, or what the law says, with the source;
- **what to do**: the question to ask the employer (in writing), the register to check, or the clause to have
  changed;
- **how serious**, one of three:
  - **stop**: a scam sign or something unlawful; don't go on until it is cleared;
  - **ask first**: often harmless, sometimes not; get the answer before applying or signing;
  - **note**: worth knowing when comparing; no action needed.

Rules:

- Evidence or nothing. A signal without a quote or a source isn't written down. Rumours (a 牛客 thread, one
  angry review) are labelled as such, with how many people say it.
- Signals, not verdicts. A 驻场 role can be a fine first job; a big 年终 can be real. Say what is uncertain.
- No made-up figures: no "usual" bonus months, no penalty amounts, no salary bands without a source.
- **Not legal advice.** You are reading the text against the statute and official guidance. For a dispute,
  money already lost, or a contract with large sums, point to the free official help (below) or a lawyer.
- The user decides. Give the facts and the questions; don't argue them out of a role they want.

## 2. Stop signs: scams and pay-to-work (anywhere)

- **They ask for money.** A fee, deposit, training cost, uniform, "materials", a medical check paid to the
  recruiter, or a loan to pay for training before the job (培训贷). In China, an employer hiring someone may not
  hold their ID or other papers, ask them for a guarantee, or take money from them under any name
  (劳动合同法 第九条). In Australia, Scamwatch's advice is never to pay up front by bank transfer, PayID or
  crypto to get or keep a job.
- **Task scams**: paid to like, rate or "optimise" things online, then asked to pay to unlock tasks or
  commissions. Scamwatch counts these as the largest kind of job scam, with the largest losses.
- **The shape of a scam**: contact out of the blue; a quick move to WhatsApp, Telegram or a personal WeChat;
  hiring with no real interview; high pay for little work and no experience; a company that exists only in its
  own ads; asking for bank details, ID scans or a "verification" payment before an offer.
- **Pressure**: "decide today", "only two places left", threats when the user hesitates.

Tell the user plainly, and how to report it: the platform it came through, Scamwatch in Australia, the police
(反诈 96110 / the 国家反诈中心 app) in China; if money has gone, their bank at once.

## 3. The posting: is the role what its title says?

- **The work doesn't match the title.** Read the duties, not the title: "管培生", "储备干部", "客户经理",
  "business development" whose duties are finding customers and meeting sales targets is a sales job. Pay
  written as a low base plus commission (底薪 + 提成) confirms it. Sales can be a fine choice; it must be chosen
  knowingly.
- **Vague duties**: a list of qualities ("抗压能力强", "接受加班", "适应快节奏") and no tasks. Ask what a typical
  week is and what the first three months' targets are.
- **Outsourcing and dispatch under a brand's name.** The posting names a famous company, but the contract is
  with another one: a 外包 / 人力资源 / 科技服务 company, a "project" role, 驻场 (working at the client's site),
  or 劳务派遣. Ask **which company signs the labour contract and pays the social insurance**: that is the
  employer, whatever the badge says. Dispatch is allowed only for temporary (at most six months), auxiliary or
  stand-in positions (劳动合同法 第六十六条), so a core engineering role filled by dispatch is a signal on its
  own. Outsourced roles differ in pay, benefits, promotion and how the brand's own teams treat them; they can
  still be a way in. Say which it is.
- **Hours**: 大小周, 单休, "弹性工作" with no hours, or "接受出差" with no share given. Ask for the real schedule.
- **Mismatched asks**: "应届" with "三年以上经验", a pay range so wide it means nothing, only "面议", no city.
- **A posting that never closes**: the same role reposted for months can mean people keep leaving. Check the
  posting's history in the tracker's scans (first and last seen), and reviews.
- **Australia, unpaid work**: an unpaid internship is lawful only as a vocational placement the course requires,
  or where there is no employment relationship at all; a label ("internship", "volunteer", "work experience")
  doesn't make it lawful. An unpaid trial is lawful only for as long as reasonably needed to show the skills the
  job needs; a trial that covers rostered shifts is work and must be paid (Fair Work Ombudsman, "Unpaid work").
- **Australia, a graduate asked for an ABN** to do an employee's work (set hours, the company's tools, under
  direction): possible sham contracting, which takes away minimum pay, super and leave. Ask why, and check with
  the Fair Work Ombudsman.
- **Visa**: a job that needs more hours than the student visa allows in term (48 a fortnight), or an employer
  asking the applicant to pay for visa sponsorship (unlawful in Australia; check Home Affairs' page on it).

## 4. The company

- **Is it what it says?** China: 国家企业信用信息公示系统 for the registered name, capital, status (存续 or not),
  abnormal operations (经营异常) and penalties; 中国执行信息公开网 for 被执行人 and 失信 records; 中国裁判文书网
  for labour disputes (a pattern of unpaid-wage cases matters; one case doesn't). Australia: ASIC Connect and
  ABN Lookup for the company, its status and age.
- **Is the business, and the team, healthy?** Recent layoffs, hiring freezes, unpaid wages or offers withdrawn,
  from reputable news; for a listed company, its latest results. A new business line or a team just set up is
  the first to be cut when money is short: ask how long the team has existed and how many people it has.
- **What people inside say**: 脉脉, 看准网, Glassdoor, 牛客 面经 and offer threads. Read many, discount the
  angriest and the happiest, and report the pattern with counts ("12 of 30 reviews in 2026 mention unpaid
  overtime"), never one quote as the truth.
- Write what you find into the company's About as a point with topic "Risks" (in the language `show` names for
  the company's research), each with its source. `fill` replaces the whole About, so `show` the company first
  and send its summary and every existing point back with the new one.

## 5. The offer, the 三方 and the contract

Read the whole document, clause by clause, with the user. Then a table: the clause, what it says, what the law
or the usual practice is (with source), and what to ask.

### Pay: what is guaranteed and what isn't

- **Split the 年包.** Monthly base × months paid; bonus (年终) and how it is decided; one-off amounts (签字费,
  relocation), and whether they must be paid back if the user leaves early; shares or options, with the vesting
  schedule. **Only what the contract or the offer letter commits to is pay**: a bonus "usually 3–6 months" said
  in an interview is a hope. Ask for it in writing, or count it as zero when comparing.
- **Options in a private company** may never be worth anything; say so, and count them separately.
- **Social insurance and housing fund (五险一金)**: ask on what base they are paid (the actual salary, or the
  local minimum base) and the housing-fund rate. The difference is real money every month, and in later
  benefits.
- **Australia**: superannuation is paid on top of wages at the Super Guarantee rate (check the ATO's page for the
  current rate); check the pay against the award or minimum wage with the Fair Work pay calculator; an offer
  quoted "including super" is less than it looks.

### China: the labour contract (劳动合同法, as amended 2012)

- **A written contract** must be signed: from one month to a year after starting without one, the employer owes
  double wages each month (第八十二条). Don't start without one.
- **The trial period (试用期)** depends on the contract's length: under three months, none; three months to a
  year, at most one month; one to three years, at most two months; three years or more, or open-ended, at most
  six months; only one trial period per employer and employee (第十九条). Trial pay may not be below 80% of the
  contract wage or the lowest grade for the same role, nor below the local minimum wage (第二十条).
- **Penalties the employee pays (违约金)** are allowed in only two cases: a service period tied to special
  training the employer paid for, capped at the training cost (第二十二条), and breach of confidentiality or a
  non-compete (第二十三条). Any other penalty on the employee is not allowed (第二十五条).
- **A non-compete (竞业限制)** may cover only senior managers, senior technical staff and others with
  confidentiality duties, lasts at most two years after leaving, and must be paid for monthly during it
  (第二十三条, 第二十四条). A blanket non-compete on an ordinary graduate, or one with no pay, is a signal to ask
  about.
- **The employer is the company that signs.** Check its name against the brand, and that the city, the role and
  the pay in the contract are what was offered.

### China: the 三方 (就业协议)

- It is an agreement between the student, the employer and the university about future employment, signed while
  the user is still a student; it is not the labour contract. Once the labour contract is signed, the contract
  governs.
- It often carries a penalty for breaking it (违约金). Practice is commonly a few thousand yuan, but there is no
  single legal cap: read the amount and the conditions in the text, and the university's own rules on
  re-issuing a 三方 after breaking one.
- **Before signing**: compare it with the other offers in play (the tracker's offers); don't sign a 三方 just to
  "lock in" a safe offer without knowing the cost of breaking it; ask for anything promised (city, team, 户口,
  housing, 编制, pay) to be written into the 三方's notes (备注) or the offer letter. A promise not in writing
  usually can't be enforced.
- **An offer withdrawn by the employer** after a signed 三方: the employer is in breach; the user should keep the
  documents and go to their university's 就业指导中心 first.

### Deciding between offers

When the user has two or more, compare them in one table, facts only: the first year's guaranteed cash; the
uncertain parts, separately; hours; the city's cost of living; the team and business line's health; whether it is
the brand's own contract or outsourced; the trial period; penalties and non-competes; and, from the direction
skill (`../direction/SKILL.md`), where each leads in 3–5 years and what it keeps open. Then say which you would
take and why, and let the user choose.

## 6. Where findings go, and where to get help

- A role's signals: `hunt.py note <company>/<role> "<finding, with source>"` so they stay with the role.
- A company's: a "Risks" point in its About (section 4).
- An offer's: the table in the chat; the key points as a note on the role.
- Free official help: China, the university's 就业指导中心, and the local 劳动人事争议仲裁委员会 (labour arbitration
  costs nothing to apply for) or the 12333 hotline; Australia, the Fair Work Ombudsman (pay and conditions),
  Scamwatch (scams), the university's careers and legal services, Home Affairs (visas).
